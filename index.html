Project 05 — Statistical Arbitrage

Cointegration & Kalman Filter Pairs Trading

Systematic pairs trading framework: Engle-Granger + Johansen dual cointegration testing, Kalman filter for dynamic hedge ratio, walk-forward backtesting across 190 pairs. The OOS failure of IS-cointegrated pairs is the central finding — not a limitation.

Cointegration
Dual-Test Screening Framework
Engle-Granger tests OLS residual stationarity (direction-dependent). Johansen tests cointegration rank jointly (direction-independent). Pairs confirmed by both are more robust — the only Johansen-confirmed pair (EOG/WFC) had near-zero OOS loss vs deep drawdowns for non-confirmed pairs.
Kalman Filter
Dynamic Hedge Ratio vs Rolling OLS
Two-state Kalman filter estimates α and β as latent states evolving over time. No arbitrary window choice. δ parameter controls adaptation speed (1e-5 → near-static, 1e-3 → fast). Smoother than rolling OLS and avoids window-end artifacts at structural breaks.
Key Finding
IS Cointegration Failed OOS — Why
All 8 IS-cointegrated pairs had negative OOS Sharpe. KO/PEP broke during 2022 inflation (different pricing power). SLB pairs broke during energy transition. Confirms: statistical tests are backward-looking. Live pairs trading requires quarterly re-screening and regime detection overlay.

Credit Default Model — Merton vs Altman vs XGBoost

Structural, score-based, and ML approaches to credit default prediction on 46 companies (33 healthy, 13 distressed). Merton model outperforms XGBoost because market prices are forward-looking — the central insight of structural credit models.

Merton Structural Model
Equity as Call Option on Firm Assets
Implements Merton (1974): solve two-equation system (BS call + Ito's lemma) via Newton-Raphson to back out implied asset value V and asset vol σ_V from equity price + debt. Distance-to-Default DD = (ln(V/K) + drift·T) / (σ_V·√T). PD = N(−DD).
Why Merton Beats ML
Market Prices Are Forward-Looking
Equity volatility and market cap embed market expectations about future distress before accounting statements reflect it. Altman uses backward-looking ratios. XGBoost overfits at N=46 (CV-AUC=1.0 vs test AUC=0.78). At scale, XGBoost would win — but structural models dominate on small samples.
SHAP Analysis
log(Market Cap) and Equity Vol Drive Predictions
SHAP (not XGBoost gain) ranks log_market_cap #1 and equity_vol_annual #2 — consistent with Merton intuition. XGBoost gain incorrectly ranks roa #1 due to high-cardinality bias. Altman Z-score fails for utilities: capital-intensive regulated firms look "distressed" by design.
